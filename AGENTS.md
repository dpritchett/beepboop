# beepboop

Go-based sound pipeline lab for designing, previewing, processing, and
exporting reusable synthetic and voice-based audio assets.

## Ground Rules

- Use TDD for synthesis primitives, effects, WAV reading/writing, preset and
  recipe resolution, Piper integration seams, and CLI behavior.
- Keep steps small and commits focused.
- Inject ambient state: filesystem paths, process spawning, clocks, and player commands should be passed through seams.
- Keep Piper, playback tools, and encoders optional. Report missing tools
  cleanly instead of making core tests depend on them.
- Prefer deterministic generated samples over checked-in opaque binaries while the sound design is changing.
- Use exported WAV/MP3 assets only as distribution artifacts once presets stabilize.
- Keep Timerbox integration one-way: Timerbox may consume exported sounds, but beepboop should not depend on Timerbox.
- Keep the CLI thin. The durable product is reusable sound pipelines, effects,
  voice recipes, and rendered artifacts.

## Verifying audio on this host

**`ffplay` playback through WSL2 is not a reliable measurement channel.** It
judders and pops in the first seconds of any stream, including a pure sine
generated by `ffmpeg` itself. Audio routes through WSL2 to Windows and
underruns early in a stream. Every artifact is reproducible on files that are
provably clean, so listening through it will send you chasing bugs that do not
exist.

Verify renders numerically instead:

```sh
beepboop inspect dist/lab/*.wav
```

- `seam` is the jump at the wrap point, `p99` the 99th percentile jump inside
  the buffer. A seamless loop has `seam` at or below `p99`.
- `maxstep` and `at` locate the single largest discontinuity. A real click
  reads orders of magnitude above `p99`; a ratio of 1.2 to 1.6 is the ordinary
  tail of a random distribution, not a defect.

For an actual listen, open the folder in Windows and play from there:

```sh
cd dist && explorer.exe .
```

Windows Media Player Legacy plays these correctly. Ask Daniel to judge
timbre; do not ask him to judge artifacts you have not first ruled out with
`inspect`. When a listener reports a timing ("about a second in"), their clock
starts when they hear sound, not when the file starts. Never pad silence and
then treat their report as impossible.

## Stop Rules

Stop and report if tests fail, generated output is nondeterministic without an explicit reason, or a playback/export dependency is missing and cannot be cleanly optional.
