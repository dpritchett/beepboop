# Navigator voice lines

Handoff notes for the spaceflight navigator sim. Generated by beepboop from
`recipes/navigator.json`; regenerate rather than hand-editing the audio.

## What you get

21 files, one per slug, named `<slug>.wav`. The filenames match your event
slugs exactly, so `sounds/${slug}.wav` resolves without a lookup table.

19 are one-shot voice lines; 2 are continuous flight beds that must be played
on repeat. See the next section for those.

- PCM16 mono WAV, 22050 Hz
- 0.63s to 1.95s each, 836K total
- Normalized to a 0.72 peak, except `view-error` at 0.88

built to cut through instead of blending in.

## Flight beds (loops, not one-shots)

| slug | what | peak |
|---|---|---|
| `flight-slow` | airflow bed while the camera is moving | 0.26 |
| `flight-fast` | the same bed with the filter opened up, for boost | 0.38 |

These two are different from everything else in the set:

- **Play them looped.** Both are exactly 2.000s and wrap seamlessly. Set
  `loop = true` and let them run while the camera moves; do not retrigger
  them per frame or per input.
- **They are one sound at two speeds, not two sounds.** `flight-fast` is
  `flight-slow` with the tone filter opened from 0.16 to 0.30 and dropped to
  two poles, over the same 90/180 Hz partials and the same noise seed. That is
  deliberate: you can crossfade between them and it reads as one airflow
  changing speed rather than a swap. A gain-only ramp between the two sounds
  right; a hard cut works but wastes the design.
- **They are already leveled to sit under the voice lines** at 0.26 and 0.38
  against the voice at 0.72. Do not normalize them up to match.

Loop seams are verified numerically rather than by ear:
`beepboop inspect dist/navigator/flight-*.wav` reports `seam` at or below
`p99`, and `maxstep` in line with `p99`, which together mean no click at the
wrap or anywhere else.

## Two things to plan around

**Speech is the wrong shape for your high-frequency events.** `select`,
`capture`, `release`, and `fast-on`/`fast-off` fire on every click, keypress,
and bumper tap, and these lines run about a second each. Held shift or rapid
selection will stack them on top of each other. Either cut them off on
retrigger, or ask for short synthetic blips for those five and keep speech for
the deliberate actions. beepboop generates both.

**836K of WAV is heavy for a browser.** These are uncompressed on purpose:
WAV is what beepboop exports today. If the payload matters, say so and it gets
an MP3 or Ogg export path.

## Asking for changes

Every line, its effects chain, and its level live in
`recipes/navigator.json` in the beepboop repo. Changing wording, adding a
slug, or retuning the grit is a one-line edit plus a rebake. Ask for what you
want in plain language; do not edit the WAVs.

Regenerate with:

```sh
export BEEPBOOP_VOICE_MODEL=~/.local/share/piper/voices/en_US-lessac-medium.onnx
beepboop bake recipes/navigator.json <your-repo>/path/to/audio
```

Output is deterministic: the same recipe and voice model produce byte-identical
files every run, so a rebake shows up in your diff only when the recipe
actually changed.
